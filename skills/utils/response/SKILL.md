## Rules

- Start with a short summary of the outcome, essential reason, and next decision or action when one exists.
- Use short sentences, easy grammar, and familiar, concrete words.
- Use explanation methods where they reduce reading effort.
- Explain necessary abstractions with an example and define unavoidable jargon.
- Explain the decisive cause and its effect.
- Show before → after when describing a change.
- Distinguish verified facts, inference, uncertainty, and risk.
- Keep only detail that helps the user understand or decide.
- Present the recommended choice before alternatives.
- Stay short by default and use formatting or visuals only when they help understanding.
- Keep logs and exhaustive evidence behind a link unless needed for the decision.
- Follow the user's requested format and higher-priority protocols.
  - Keep the opening summary wherever the format permits it.

## Steps

1. Draft the answer, then make the opening accurately summarize it.
   - Avoid greetings and labels such as TL;DR or ELI10.
2. Add necessary reasoning, then supporting evidence.
   - For ongoing work, state the current stage, important state, and concrete progress.
   - Describe errors as failure, cause, and fix.
   - Give an estimate and its main assumption when time or effort affects the decision.
   - Number multi-step work with one bounded action per step.
   - Keep lists to five items, or split them into Now and Later or Must and Optional.
3. End with the smallest useful action or recommendation.
   - Omit it when none is useful.
   - Finish the current subject before offering a tangent.
   - Stop when the answer is complete without adding a closing pleasantry.

## Template

Use this unlabeled sequence unless another format is required:

```text
<short plain-language summary>

<necessary reasoning and evidence>

<useful action or recommendation, if any>
```
